# Digital Product Playbook (living document)

Source: course sessions (screenshots). Sessions 2 and 3 are in. Session 4 will be added.
Rule: every product we build must pass all 5 Unlocks and have a one-sentence Product Promise.

---

## 0. Product formats
| Format | What it is | Best when | Example from the course |
|---|---|---|---|
| eBook | Written guide that teaches how to do something | Result can be reached by reading and doing | Faceless eBook on farming gold in a game |
| Digital Assets | Ready-made files (templates, planners, design packs) | The product saves time or money | Budget tracker, about $7,000/month |
| Pre-recorded program | Video lessons at own pace | Person needs to SEE it done | "Pencil Drawing Made Easy" |
| Digital Mind | AI that answers in an expert's voice | Ongoing access to an expert | AskIman |

Note: value is what sells, not page count. Start with eBooks (our decision), add other formats later.

## 1. Unlock 1 — Pain (identify the problem)
- Do NOT look for a brilliant original idea. One useful idea is enough.
- Two problem types: **Pleasure** (nice to have) vs **Pain** (hurts right now). People run from pain harder than toward pleasure.
- Pain lives in 3 pillars: **Wealth, Health, Relationships**.
- Test: how often does the problem remind the person? Toothache (every meal, every talk, every night) beats whiter teeth (once or twice a week).

**Check:** Is this a pain, not a pleasure? Which pillar? How often does it remind them?

## 2. Unlock 2 — Worsening Pain
- **Consistent pain:** stays level, bearable, people postpone.
- **Worsening pain:** gets worse over time, creates urgency, people actively search and pay.
- Toothache stages: Sensitive → Dull ache → Throbbing (calling every dentist until one can see you today).
- Also measure the SPEED of worsening. Faulty car battery (fast) beats windscreen chip (slow).
- Painful + quickly worsening = more valuable = people pay more.

**Check:** Does it get worse? How fast? What is the stage 1 / 2 / 3 of this problem?

## 3. Unlock 3 — Transformation (Point A → Point B)
- **Point A** includes four things: how they feel, what they fear, what they avoid, how they see themselves.
- **Point B:** the ideal result (usually mirrored: the opposite of A).
- Football example: A = afraid to give up football, sitting out games, thinks playing days are over. B = full match without pain, back in the team, sees self as a player.
- Why people buy: the information is already **synthesised and packaged**, so they skip searching, piecing together and trial and error.
- Done-for-you beats a stack of books (weeks of research + planning + a month of trial and error).
- AI tools can: find and rank painful worsening problems, map the transformation (stage / goal / description), and build the product.

**Check:** Write A (4 parts) and B (3 parts). Is the product the bridge, packaged and done-for-you?

## 4. Unlock 4 — Specificity
- Ladder: Broad (Parents) → Narrower (Dads) → Specific (Divorced dads with teenage sons).
- Goal: the reader thinks "they are talking to me".
- Balance: specific enough that the right person feels seen, common enough that plenty of people have the problem. A handful of people worldwide is too narrow.
- Good examples: new first-time postpartum moms and bedtime without rigid schedules; dual working parents cooking dinners without evening chopping.
- Bad example: "Busy moms: drop pounds fast with 30-minute routines" (competes with every weight-loss product for moms).
- Broad is fine for big corporations. We only need a few buyers.

**Check:** Name the exact person in one line. Would they say "that's me"? Are there plenty of them?

## 5. Unlock 5 — Speed
- Two eBooks, same price and cover: 20 pages to the result vs 200 pages. People buy the 20-page one. They buy a **result**, not pages.
- Two reasons: **Doubt** (seeing a result reassures them) and **Logic** (shorter time to result makes the decision easier).
- Limits: a 5-minute fix will not be paid for; an unrealistic timeline kills trust.
- Method: ask how fast someone can REALISTICALLY see a result, then design a **smaller first win** they reach sooner. Quick first win builds trust, trust builds value.

**Check:** What is the first win and when does the reader reach it? Is the timeline believable?

---

## 6. The Product Promise (final sentence)
Four parts: **Who** (Specificity) · **The Problem** (Pain + Worsening) · **The Result** (Transformation) · **The Time** (Speed)

> I help **[who]**, with **[a problem that's getting worse]**, get to **[Point B]** in **[how long]**.

Example: *I help first-time parents of 4 to 6-month-olds, whose nights get harder every week, get their baby sleeping through the night in 7 nights.*

---

## 7. Our build workflow (every product)
1. Pick niche → find a PAIN in Wealth / Health / Relationships (Unlock 1).
2. Confirm it WORSENS and how fast (Unlock 2).
3. Map Point A and Point B (Unlock 3).
4. Narrow to one specific person (Unlock 4).
5. Define the first win and a realistic time (Unlock 5).
6. Write the Product Promise sentence. If any part is vague, go back.
7. Build the product: chapters follow the A→B map, first win in chapter 1, done-for-you templates and checklists.
8. Validate with the checklist (5/5) before publishing.

## 8. Safety rules (our own)
- No medical, legal or financial advice that promises results. Health examples in the course are for teaching the framework only.
- Never invent numbers, income claims or testimonials.
- Facts need real sources.

## 10. Session 3 — Value, faceless proof, formats, hosting

**Numbering note:** Session 3's recap lists the unlocks as 1 Transformation, 2 Pain, 3 Worsening Pain, 4 Specificity, 5 Speed. Session 2 had Pain first. Same five ideas, different numbers. We use the Session 2 order.

### 10.1 A buyer only cares about 2 things: The Problem → The Fix
- Point A → Point B examples: "I can't fall asleep" → "I sleep through the night"; "I've never cooked anything" → "I can cook 5 meals for the week"; "Spreadsheets scare me" → "I built my budget in an afternoon".
- Story: engaged, quotes for venue, dress, photographer and food, no idea where the money goes → a Google Sheets wedding planner (9 tabs, instant download).
- When buying, people click the first option that looks like it will fix the problem. They do not check who made it, how they look, or their followers.

### 10.2 Intrinsic value vs borrowed value
- **Intrinsic value:** value the thing has by itself, purely from what it does. Example: Google Maps (nobody asks who drew the roads).
- **Borrowed value:** extra value borrowed from a person. Example: a signed Ronaldo jersey, same fabric, much higher price. Buying from a famous expert is partly paying for the expert.
- Most of the digital product market runs on intrinsic value, which is why faceless works.
- The course says the next main session covers how to use borrowed value (to be added).

### 10.3 Faceless examples (course claims, not verified by us)
- **Avada:** best-selling website template on ThemeForest, 800,000+ sales, $60M+ revenue, two-person team, buyers know the product, not the people.
- **Visualize Value:** one black-and-white graphic a day became a brand, then courses and a one-page planner. January 2020: $58 in the account; end of 2020: $1M+ sales, no ads.

- **Store page:** product page in minutes, no website, no coding.
- **Every format:** ebooks, video programs, digital minds, templates, plus communities and memberships.
- **Payments:** buyers pay through Whop, money goes to your account; one-time, monthly or yearly; free trials; instalments; card, buy-now-pay-later, crypto; 100+ payment methods across 195 countries.

### 10.6 Our notes (not from the course)
- Our influencer-partner model is **borrowed value**: the partner's audience trust adds value. The product must still pass all 5 unlocks, because trust alone does not keep buyers.
- Apply "Problem → Fix" to every title and first line of the sales page.
- Prefer formats we can build ourselves: eBook + templates/spreadsheets first, video programs via slides later.
- Treat all income figures as marketing claims until verified.
